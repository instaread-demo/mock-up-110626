Anthony Joshua and Tyson Fury spent the best part of a decade as Britain’s two biggest heavyweights without sharing a ring. The fight is finally set for the Principality Stadium on 11 December, and the AJ vs Fury odds frame it as a meeting of two men in their late 30s with their legacies tied to one night.

The winner takes the vacant World Boxing Association (WBA) ‘super’ title, which Oleksandr Usyk gave up in June 2026. What the loser forfeits is harder to measure, and it is not the same for both.

What Joshua Stands to Lose

Joshua got this fight on his own terms. Netflix and Saudi backer Turki Alalshikh pushed for Madison Square Garden, and Joshua turned down extra money to keep the bout in Britain. If he loses in front of a home crowd he insisted on, the setting is his own choice.

His record already carries the dents. Joshua is 30-4, and two of those defeats came inside the distance, against Andy Ruiz Jr in June 2019 and Daniel Dubois at Wembley in September 2024. He has also been put down in fights he went on to win. Kristian Prenga dropped him twice in the first round in Jeddah in July 2026 before Joshua knocked him out in the second.

Time is the other factor. Eddie Hearn has said 2026 is probably Joshua’s final year in boxing. A defeat would close a career that included two world heavyweight title reigns with a loss in the domestic fight the British public wanted most.

What Fury Stands to Lose

Fury’s record is the cleaner of the two, which is exactly why it is more exposed. He is 36-2-1 and has never been stopped in 39 professional fights. Both defeats came against Usyk, on a split decision in May 2024 and unanimously in the rematch that December.

That record underpins his long-held claim to be the best British heavyweight of his generation. It rests on a win over Wladimir Klitschko in Dusseldorf in 2015 and a draw and two stoppage wins against Deontay Wilder between 2018 and 2021. Losing to Joshua would hand that argument to the rival he has spent years dismissing.

Fury has also walked away more than once. He announced his retirement in January 2025, then returned with a 12-round unanimous decision over Arslanbek Makhmudov at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium in April 2026. He followed it by stopping Mariusz Wach in the seventh round in Thailand in July. A loss in Cardiff would make this comeback look like one fight too many.

Who Has More Riding on Cardiff?

Joshua has already shown his career can absorb a defeat. He lost his titles to Ruiz at Madison Square Garden in June 2019 and won them back in Saudi Arabia six months later. His standing has survived four losses because he kept taking the biggest fights available, including two against Usyk.

Fury’s standing rests on the opposite. Outside the two Usyk defeats, nobody has beaten him in almost 18 years as a professional. A Joshua win would rewrite the order of their generation, while a Fury win would mostly confirm what he has said about it for years.

That makes Fury the man with more to lose. Joshua is chasing a third world heavyweight title reign and a way to finish his career on his own terms. Fury is protecting a record that only one man has ever spoiled.

Joshua is also the bigger puncher on paper, with 27 knockouts from 30 wins against Fury’s 25 from 36. Those numbers make the method-of-victory markets worth a closer look, and an odds calculator will show what a Joshua stoppage or a Fury points win would return. Fury has beaten Derek Chisora three times and stopped Dillian Whyte in front of 94,000 at Wembley in 2022. No British heavyweight has beaten him yet.
