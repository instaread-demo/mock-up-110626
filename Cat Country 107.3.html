Garth Brooks Is Bringing Back a Piece of His Legendary ’90s Career

Garth Brooks isn't just launching another tour — he's revisiting one of the most iconic chapters of his career.

The country superstar's Blame It All on My Roots Tour will take fans back to the arena shows that helped make him a household name, complete with a familiar stage setup and plans to capture the experience on a brand-new live album.

Bringing Back a Piece of Country Music History

At the heart of the tour is Brooks' famous in-the-round drum pod, the centerpiece of his legendary 1990s arena shows.

Read More: Garth Brooks Got Rejected by the Same Record Label That Built His Career

The stage became synonymous with Brooks' high-energy performances and was immortalized on Double Live, which remains the best-selling live album in music history.

Ahead of the announcement, Brooks shared a nostalgic teaser on social media, walking toward the drum pod before asking, “How long has it been, old girl? Thirty years?”

The video then flashed back to footage from his iconic 1996-98 World Tour before Brooks smiled and remarked that the stage looked “a little dusty.”

Read More: Garth Brooks Played a Classic at His Grand Ole Opry Debut

“You know what?” he added. “Maybe it's time. Maybe it's time we put you back to work.”

Going Back to Where It All Began

Brooks says returning to arenas is about more than nostalgia.

“Going back into the arenas is about putting the stadium show in a box,” he said in a statement. “The excitement gets multiplied by the intimacy. Every seat is a great seat. This is personal.”

The Blame It All on My Roots Tour will feature both end-stage and in-the-round seating, recreating the concert experience that helped define Brooks' rise to country music superstardom.

He's Also Making a New Live Album

The tour will serve another purpose beyond celebrating the past.

Read More: Garth Brooks Considering Selling His Catalog For a Whopping $2 Billion

Brooks plans to record the shows for a new live project titled Killer Live, giving fans a new concert album nearly three decades after Double Live became a landmark release.

The tour kicks off with back-to-back shows at Indianapolis' Gainbridge Fieldhouse on Aug. 21 and 22, with additional dates announced at any given moment.

20 Songs Garth Brooks Has Played Live the Most Times

Garth Brooks has been filling the biggest arenas in the U.S. and the world since he launched his country career in 1989. There are 20 songs that have emerged as fan favorites, and Brooks has played some of them hundreds of times over the years.

Data reflected runs through March 2024

Gallery Credit: Sterling Whitaker

Terry Wyatt, Getty Images

No. 20: "The Fever"

Garth Brooks had a disappointing chart showing with his cover of Aerosmith's "The Fever," which originally appeared on their 1993 Get a Grip album. He missed the Top 10 with his version from 1995's Fresh Horses, but he still likes to perform the song live.
Times Played: 73
First Played: Nov. 16, 1995, at NDR Studio, Hamburg
Last Played: Sept. 11, 2022, at Croke Park in Dublin, Ireland

Kevork Dzansezian, Getty Images

No. 19: "American Pie"

Brooks' influences stem from both country and classic rock, and it's not unusual for him to mix those influences in his live show. His epic cover of Don McLean's "American Pie" is a song that's gotten quite a bit of play in his live gigs over the years.
Times Played: 90
First Played: Sept. 22, 1994, at Palau San Jordi in Barcelona, Spain
Last Played: Jan. 4, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Kevin Winter, Getty Images

No. 18: "Standing Outside the Fire"

Brooks scored a No. 3 hit with "Standing Outside the Fire" from his In Pieces album in 1993. The song has gone on to become a frequent staple of his live shows in the years since.
Times Played: 96
First Played: Aug. 3, 1993, at the Tacoma Dome in Tacoma, Wash.
﻿Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Rick Diamond, Getty Images

No. 17: "If Tomorrow Never Comes"

Brooks scored one of his most enduring hits with "If Tomorrow Never Comes," hitting No. 1 from his self-titled debut album in 1989. The powerful song has been an important part of his setlist live over the years.
Times Played: 99
First Played: July 14, 1990, at Richland Carnival Grounds in Richland, Penn.
Last Played: Sept. 2, 2023, at Caesars Superdome in New Orleans

John Medina, Getty Images

No. 16: "Two Pina Coladas"

Brooks reached No. 1 on the charts with "Two Pina Coladas," the third single from his Sevens album in 1997. The song is perfect for his high-energy live shows, and it's been a top-played song over the decades.
Times Played: 131
First Played: May 16, 1997, at Croke Park in Dublin, Ireland
Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Rick Diamond, Getty Images

No. 15: "We Shall Be Free"

No other country artist has leaned as much on songs with big messages, and Brooks has always been especially fond of "We Shall Be Free," a No. 12 hit from his 1992 album, The Chase.
Times Played: 134
First Played: July 19, 1992, at America West Arena in Phoenix, Ariz.
﻿Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Jason Kempin, Getty Images

No. 14: "Much Too Young (To Feel This Damn Old)"

Brooks launched his career with this tale of hard living on the rodeo circuit, which served as the first single from his 1989 self-titled debut album. It's been a live favorite ever since.
Times Played: 138
First Played: June 8, 1989, at Fan Fair in Nashville
Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Ethan Miller, Getty Images

No. 13: "Papa Loved Mama"

"Papa Loved Mama" is a fun, up-tempo song that's been at home in Brooks' live shows ever since he released it as the fourth single from Ropin' the Wind in 1991.
Times Played: 150
First Played: Sept. 20, 1991, at Reunion Arena in Dallas, Texas
Last Played: July 23, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Rick Diamond, Getty Images

No. 12: "The Beaches of Cheyenne"

Brooks scored a No. 1 country hit with a supernatural edge to it with "The Beaches of Cheyenne" in 1995. The song is a staple of his live concerts going all the way back to before it was released.
Times Played: 154
First Played: July 19, 1992, at America West Arena in Phoenix, Ariz.
﻿Last Played: Dec. 13, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Mark Wilson, Getty Images

No. 11: "Ain't Goin' Down ('Til the Sun Comes Up)"

Brooks' monster No. 1 hit from his 1993 In Pieces album is such a natural fit for his live shows that it's hard to believe it's not one of his all-time Top 10. It falls just outside of that at No. 11, but he's still played it plenty of times over the years.
Times Played: 161
First Played: July 19, 1992, at America West Arena in Phoenix, Ariz.
﻿Last Played: Sept 2, 2023, at Caesars Superdome in New Orleans

Shannon Finney, Getty Images

No. 10: "Shameless"

Brooks once again re-visited his classic rock roots for this Billy Joel cover, which reached No. 1 when he released it as the second single from his landmark Ropin' the Wind album. It's one of his most popular live songs.
Times Played: 165
First Played: May 4, 1991, at McArthur Court in Eugene, Ore.
Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Theo Wargo, Getty Images

No. 9: "That Summer"

Brooks actually co-wrote "That Summer" with his now-ex-wife, Sandy Mahl, along with songwriter Pat Alger. The song reached No. 1 when he released it as the fourth single from The Chase, and it's been a prominent part of his live shows since.
Times Played: 177
First Played: July 19, 1992, at America West Arena in Phoenix, Ariz.
﻿Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Jason Kempin, Getty Images

No. 8: "Unanswered Prayers"

Brooks landed a No. 1 hit with "Unanswered Prayers," the second single from 1990's No Fences album. The song's universal message has made it one of his most popular songs to perform live.
Times Played: 181
First Played: Aug. 8, 1991, at Great Woods Center for the Performing Arts in Mansfield, Mass.
Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Kevin Winter, Getty Images

No. 7: "Rodeo"

Brooks missed No. 1 with "Rodeo," the first single from Ropin' the Wind. But the song has gone on to be one of his most important songs to perform live at his concerts over the years.
Times Played: 192
First Played: Aug. 8, 1991, at Great Woods Center for the Performing Arts in Mansfield, Mass.
Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Ethan Miller, Getty Images

No. 6: "Two of a Kind, Workin' on a Full House"

Brooks scored his fifth No. 1 hit with "Two of a Kind, Workin' on a Full House," from 1990's No Fences. The song is a staple of his live concerts even today.
Times Played: 193
First Played: July 14, 1989, at Allentown Bluegrass Fest
Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Rich Fury, Getty Images

No. 5: "Callin' Baton Rouge"

Brooks landed a massive hit with his version of "Callin' Baton Rouge," which the Oak Ridge Boys had previously recorded. His cut on the song reached No. 2 from his In Pieces album, and it's a show-stopping part of his live concerts.
Times Played: 194
First Played: July 19, 1992, at America West Arena in Phoenix, Ariz.
Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Daniel Boczarski, Getty Images

No. 4: "The River"

Brooks landed his ninth No. 1 hit with "The River," from his landmark Ropin' the Wind album. The song's uplifting message, coupled with his passionate performance, have made it a cornerstone of his concerts.
﻿Times Played: 203
First Played: Sept. 20, 1991, at Reunion Arena in Dallas, Texas
Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Ethan Miller, Getty Images

No. 3: "The Thunder Rolls"

"The Thunder Rolls" gave Garth Brooks his sixth No. 1 hit in 1990, and he's performed it as a matter of course at his live gigs over the decades.
Times Played: 211
First Played: July 14, 1989, at Allentown Bluegrass Festival
Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Ethan Miller, Getty Images

No. 2: "The Dance"

"The Dance" gave Brooks his second No. 1 hit in 1990, and it's one of the most definitive songs of his entire career. His emotionally affecting performance has held audiences spellbound at his live shows.
Times Played: 217
First Played: July 14, 1989, at Allentown Bluegrass Fest 1989
Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas

Kevin Winter, Getty Images

No. 1: "Friends in Low Places"

Brooks scored his third No. 1 hit with "Friends in Low Places," the lead single from his sophomore album, No Fences. The rowdy song with its "lost verse" has become one of his most important songs, and it's the song Brooks has performed live more than any other song over the years.
Times Played: 236
First Played: July 14, 1989, at Allentown Bluegrass Fest 1989
Last Played: Dec. 16, 2023, at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas
